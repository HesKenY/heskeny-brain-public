Strategic roadmap
  1. 1

    Distribution: trade-specific entry points

    CHERP solo→company funnel with per-trade variants

    Each trade discipline ships as a branded mobile application across three tiers: free with ads for organic adoption by individual crew members, paid ad-unlock as a one-time in-app purchase for crews who want a clean experience, and company tether once a foreman approves a connection request — the crew's solo apps roll up into a paid company instance with shared data and office-side visibility.

    Seven trade-named PWAs (Pipe Trades, Electricians, HVAC, Fire Sprinkler, Carpentry, Concrete, Safety, General) on cherp.live. Three monetization tiers: ad-supported free, IAP ad-unlock, and company tether via the federation-v0 device-code flow. Single Supabase backend, runtime CHERP_TRADE flag, ads gated by an entitlement boolean per user.

  2. 2

    Monetization: company-tier instances on dedicated VMs

    Per-company Azure VM with partitioned storage isolation

    Each paying customer receives their own dedicated virtual machine. Their CHERP application, their data, and their files all live there — no shared infrastructure, no third-party storage to keep paying for. Office staff access company files directly from the VM through a partitioned customer drive, while the system files (CHERP and KenOS internals) are walled off and inaccessible from the customer end.

    Per-tenant Azure VM with a two-partition layout: /system holds CHERP + KenOS internals (locked, no customer-side write or read), /company is the customer's accessible vault (SMB-shared back to office staff, read/write per company role). Drive-level isolation protects the customer's data from system mutations and protects our IP from the customer end. SharePoint integration is retired; the VM's company partition is the office-side surface.

  3. 3

    Engineering capacity: autonomous maintenance

    KenOS controller with gated self-modification

    Routine engineering work — feature implementation, bug remediation, schema migrations — is performed by a local AI system, with each change verified against an evidence ledger before merge. Customers benefit from reduced release latency without expansion of engineering headcount.

    KenOS v1.2 dispatches goals to the kenai-fullstack and kenai-wolverine agents. Wolverine carries gated self-write authority over its own controller source. A cross-agent claim verifier validates every claimed change against the filesystem and git history.

  4. 4

    Capability validation: live-response training

    kenai-trainer — Pokémon Crystal completion as a Play→Break→Fix benchmark

    We validate the autonomous engineering capability by tasking a dedicated AI variant with completing a complex video game environment without human intervention. The decision loop required — observe state, attempt action, learn from outcome, refine — is the same loop used to remediate CHERP defects in production. Successful completion is a public, externally-verifiable demonstration that the system is capable of multi-step reasoning under uncertainty.

    kenai-trainer:latest drives mGBA via xdotool, produces lesson seeds (84 banked to date), maintains a separate corpus from the kenai-v5 development corpus per the no-dilution rule. The same loop pattern is reused for CHERP migrations and live remediation.

  5. 5

    Operator visibility: shared real-time access

    President access surface mirrored from operator state

    The President side and the Operator side share a single source of truth. Build progress, deployment health, customer SharePoint surfaces, and recently-shipped work all surface here automatically — no separate status meeting required to know where things stand.

    This panel is regenerated from BRAIN canonical state (project-checklist.json, master.log JSONL, claim-checks evidence) on a cron and pushed to the public mirror. No hand-curated status decks; the data is always the data.

Current bet · active hypothesis

Per-customer AI dev team on Azure

Each paid CHERP company instance gets its own Azure virtual machine running our current KenAI model, hooked directly into that company's CHERP environment. The customer's files live on a partitioned drive on the same VM — accessible to their office staff, walled off from our system code. The AI runs the day-to-day: it processes field-side data as it lands, watches the dashboards, runs the routine maintenance, and emails periodic reports straight to the customer's inbox. Funded during the test phase by Azure's startup credit allocation; if it works, every paying customer ships with what is effectively a private AI dev team without the operator team having to grow.

Per-tenant Azure VM running the current KenAI model on Ollama. Two-partition disk layout: /system holds CHERP + KenOS internals (read-only to customer); /company is the customer's accessible file vault (SMB-shared, role-gated). Tenant-scoped KenOS variant jailed to the workspace. Daily-operations loop processes inbound field data, runs scheduled maintenance, and emits report digests via SMTP. SharePoint integration retired in favor of the on-VM partitioned vault — fewer moving parts, cleaner isolation, no third-party storage dependency.

Hypothesis
A dedicated tenant-scoped AI on Azure can absorb enough daily CHERP operations + maintenance load that the operator team scales linearly with sold instances only for net-new feature work — not for per-tenant operations — and the price premium covers the per-tenant VM cost.

Probability bands · today's read

Technical viability75%
KenAI on Ollama already runs locally. An Azure GPU-VM is the same architecture in a different host; the controller already supports tenant-scoped workspace jails.
Test-phase cost containment80%
Azure-for-Startups credits cover a single GPU-VM test deployment for 6–24 months at typical sizing. Test scope is bounded to the operator's tenant.
Customer adoption at price50%
Customers value the dedicated, isolated, "your own AI" framing. Whether they'll pay enough on top of base CHERP to absorb the per-tenant VM cost (~$400–$1500/mo) is the open commercial question.
Unit economics vs shared model35%
A shared multi-tenant AI layer is materially cheaper. The dedicated-VM thesis depends on the sales premium covering the cost gap. If it doesn't, we fall back to shared with per-tenant memory + tooling jail.

How we're testing it

  • The operator's KenOS deployment is the prototype tenant — same stack we'd ship to a customer.
  • Cost is metered against the Azure-for-Startups credit allocation; burn-rate is the bounding constraint.
  • Outcome metric is operator hours of intervention per week. Goal: under 2 hours / tenant / week steady-state.
  • Feature parity check: tenant AI handles the same operational surface KenOS handles locally.

Decision gate

  • Green: stays under 2 hr/wk operator intervention and per-tenant VM cost ≤ price premium → ship as paid add-on at first customer renewal.
  • Yellow: meets one of the two thresholds → pivot to shared-pool model with dedicated-tenant memory + tooling jail.
  • Red: fails both → collapse fully to operator-driven service for the foreseeable future, revisit when local-model economics shift.
  • Decision date: tied to the first paid-customer renewal cycle, not a calendar gate.

Customer file vault

On-VM partitioned storage

Each customer's files live on their own dedicated VM, in a partition isolated from our system code. Office staff can browse and edit those files directly. Our CHERP and KenOS internals live on a separate partition the customer can't see, write to, or accidentally damage. Cleaner than a SharePoint sync, no extra subscriptions, and no shared infrastructure to worry about.

Two-partition disk layout per tenant VM. /system mounts the CHERP runtime, KenOS controller, KenAI weights, and journal/evidence files — root-owned, no customer-side mount. /company mounts the customer's vault — SMB / WebDAV exposed to the office network, role-gated by AD or a CHERP-issued credential. Nothing in /system traverses through customer-accessible paths; nothing in /company is reachable from customer-untrusted writes into /system.

/system · locked
CHERP + KenOS internals
CHERP application binaries, Supabase configuration, the KenOS controller, KenAI model weights, evidence ledger, agent jails, and the operator-side audit log. Customer end has no read or write — protects our IP, prevents accidental damage by an office user.
/company · customer-accessible
Customer file vault
Daily logs, photos, signed JSAs, project documents, drawings, submittals, sign-off archives, and the AI-generated reports. Mounted as an SMB share to the customer's office network; per-role read/write gating. This is the surface customer staff actually use day-to-day.
Isolation barrier
Why two partitions
Customer data stays customer-owned and customer-recoverable; our system code stays operator-owned and customer-tamper-proof. A misconfigured office workstation can't reach the AI weights; a model retrain on our side can't accidentally touch customer documents. Disk-level guarantee, not just a permissions guarantee.
Architecture

System overview

Field crews and office staff both interact through CHERP. Field-side data lands in the cloud database; office-side files live on the customer's own VM in a partitioned company vault their staff can browse directly. Engineering and per-tenant operations are performed by AI agents on dedicated infrastructure, with every action recorded in a unified evidence ledger.

Static PWA → Supabase (REST + RLS) + Netlify functions for the field application. Per-tenant Azure VM with /system + /company partitioned drive — KenAI + KenOS in /system, customer files in /company (SMB-shared). Local KenAI on Ollama via litellm Anthropic-compatible proxy. BRAIN holds canonical state (master.log JSONL + handoffs + evidence).

Quality assurance

Cross-agent claim verifier

Every modification claimed by an AI agent is automatically validated against the filesystem and version-control history. Claims that reference files which do not exist, or commits which were not made, are flagged and surfaced. This mechanism produces a continuous, evidence-based record of what the system actually delivered.

scripts/brain-agent-claim-checks.py — file-path and commit-SHA token verification across BRAIN, CHERP, and home roots. Per-actor breakdown. Surfaced via the Heskeny-Brain evidence dashboard.
